The Floor, in Slate & Moss.

Everything inside the main column gets rebuilt: the desk view, the desk detail, the reserve flow, and login. The desk layout itself stays exactly as it is today. This revision locks the direction and the palette, and adds a section on the chrome: four distinct ways to replace the sidebar and the breadcrumb header, which earlier revisions had left as they are. What feels rushed today, and why

Pulled from the current routes and components. Red is something every direction fixes; amber is a smaller polish item.

Filters push the map below the fold on mobile.The filter form is a vertical column with gap-16 before the desks. On a phone you scroll past four controls before you see a single desk.
Choosing a day means opening a date picker.People only ever book this week or next, Monday to Friday. A calendar popover is the wrong control for ten possible values.
Status is a 2px coloured bottom border.Free vs taken is the single most important fact on the page, and it's carried by a thin green or red line under a grey box.
The desk icon is a 64px black clip-art SVG.It dominates every tile, repeats 40 times, and is the main reason the page reads as "made in a hurry".
Filtered-out desks go red at 20% opacity."Not matching your filter" and "taken" look the same. Filtering should quiet desks, not paint them as unavailable.
Login is a bare heading and one input.No context on what a user ID looks like, and the register link says "Click here". Register's button says "Login".
Reserve uses a select for week and checkboxes for days.Two controls that don't show the actual dates. You can't see what you're booking until the toast appears.
Blocks and the wall have no spatial relationship.Column 1 is "window" and 3 is "aisle", but nothing on screen says so. The wall is a lone grey bar between block 6 and 7.
Availability squares in the modal are unlabeled.Two rows of five green/red squares with M T W Th F under them, no week label, and no dates.
Logout is a bare form submit in the sidebar.Fine functionally, but it has no confirmation and no signed-out state; the user just lands on the login page.

What every direction shares

The structural fixes inside the main column. The sidebar, the header and the breadcrumbs are decided separately, under Chrome.

  1. Day strip replaces the date picker. Mon–Fri as a segmented control at the top of the page, with a small "Next week →" text link to flip to the following week. Today is preselected; past days are shown but not offered, since their bookings are cleaned up. On phones the strip becomes five full-width cells. The URL param stays so links still work.
  2. Filters become chips under the day strip. "Free only", "Window", "Middle", "Aisle", and a "Block" chip that opens the native select. Filtering dims desks instead of colouring them red. On phones the row scrolls sideways rather than wrapping.
  3. Three desk states, always readable without colour. Free, taken (with who), yours. Each has a distinct fill, a label, and an icon or avatar. Dimmed is a fourth, purely for filtering.
  4. Desk detail is a bottom sheet on mobile and a side sheet on desktop, both built with Silk. One component, two placements; the Radix dialog and the vaul drawer go away. The panel shows the two-week availability with real dates and one primary action. The booking rules stay as they are: someone else's desk can only be borrowed for today, and only your own desk can be booked ahead.
  1. Reserve is a week calendar, not checkboxes. For your own desk only: five day tiles per week showing the date and status, tap to select, see a summary line, then confirm. For any other desk the sheet offers exactly one thing, "Reserve for today".
  2. Login is a six-character code field. Autofocus, uppercase display, inline error that names the problem. Register is the same layout with two more fields and a button that says "Create account".
  3. Logout confirms, then lands on a signed-out login screen. A short "You're signed out" line on the login page instead of a silent redirect.
  4. One motion moment per screen. The sheet sliding up, a desk lifting on hover, a tile toggling. Nothing animates on page load. Reduced motion respected.
  5. Sheets and modals come from Silk (@silk-hq/components). Every overlay in the app is a Silk sheet: the desk detail, confirmations, and the reserve form on phones. Details under Touches.

The direction

The Floor

The layout you have today, drawn properly. Blocks stay stacked in order, each a 3 by 2 grid, block 4 a single row, the wall before block 7. Each desk is a tile with an outline and a solid lower edge, so it reads as a slab on the page rather than a grey button. No perspective, no rotation, so it renders identically everywhere. Every desk carries its owner's first name, with the desk number small underneath, because the name is what people actually look for. A free desk is white with the name muted, a taken one is dark with the name in white, yours is the one moss tile, and an unclaimed desk is a dashed outline. Buttons and cards are flat; the raised edge is reserved for desks, so a raised thing always means a desk. Chairs sit on the outside of each block, which makes row 1 and row 2 tell apart at a glance. A window strip runs down the left and a dashed aisle line down the right.

Why pick it

  • Zero layout risk: the loader, the block grouping and the grid placement code stay as they are. Only the desk button and the block wrapper change.
  • The slab edge gives you the physical feel without any CSS 3D, so it looks the same in Safari, Chrome and in this document.
  • States are carried by fill and edge colour together, so they survive greyscale, and each palette is a token swap.

What it costs

  • First names only on tiles; surnames and who is borrowing from whom live in the panel. Long names truncate on narrow phones.
  • The chair rows add vertical space, so the page is about 30% taller than today.
  • Less charming than a true map, but that was the trade for keeping the layout.

The chrome: four ways to replace the sidebar and header

This supersedes every earlier "the sidebar and header stay" note. The shadcn sidebar with its five labelled groups and the breadcrumb bar above the page are the last pieces that read as a template. Below are four distinct directions. Each one drops the breadcrumb header entirely: the page title already sits at the top of the content, and secondary pages get a small "← Desks" link there instead. Each also assumes the trimmed map of the app on the right, though all four work with the nine entries as they are today.

Nine entries become three places and a menu

  • Desks. The floor plan. Unchanged.
  • Bookings. "My reservations" renamed. "Add reservation" becomes the primary button on this page (it is an action, not a place, and "Book days" already lives in the desk sheet). "Automatic reservations" becomes a second segment on the same page: Upcoming · Recurring.
  • Metrics. Unchanged.
  • You. Avatar menu on desktop, fourth tab on phones: edit profile, source code, report a bug, and sign out. The two external links stop competing with real destinations.

What goes in every option

  • The breadcrumb bar with its sidebar toggle, separator and "Desks / Edit desk" trail. Nothing above the page title.
  • Group labels ("Desks", "Reservations", "Profile", "External links"). Three destinations do not need headings.
  • The tagline "A workspace management app" under the brand. The wordmark carries a small moss mark instead, the same slab shape as a desk tile.
  • The system font in the chrome. Archivo everywhere, so the shell and the page are one thing.
  • The shadcn sidebar and, with it, the last Radix dialog. Any phone drawer that remains is a Silk sheet.

Masthead, in depth

What option 1 is like to use on every screen, and the details that decide whether the phone version feels like an app or like a website with a bar stuck to the bottom.

Desktop and tablet (768px and up)

  • One bar, 52px, sticky. Translucent paper with a hairline underneath, so the day strip and floor plan scroll under it. Its content is aligned to the same max width as the page, so the wordmark sits on the page title's left edge.
  • Wordmark. The moss mark (a tiny desk slab, 18px, with its 3px edge) and "Share a desk" in Archivo 700. It links to Desks. No tagline.
  • Three tabs. Desks, Bookings, Metrics. 13px, semibold, muted ink at rest, ink when active with a 2px moss underline flush with the bar's bottom edge. Hover darkens the text, focus shows the moss ring. They are plain links with aria-current="page", so keyboard and screen-reader behaviour comes for free.
  • Avatar menu. 26px initials on the right. Opens a small popover: a header with the name, desk and role, then Edit profile, Source code, Report a bug, and Sign out below a divider. Sign out opens the existing confirmation. Built with the native Popover API and anchored under the avatar; no dropdown library.
  • Secondary pages. Reserve, Edit desk, Edit profile have no tab of their own. The parent tab stays underlined (Reserve and Edit desk under Desks, Edit profile under nothing) and the page shows a small "← Desks" link above its title. That replaces the breadcrumb trail.
  • Tablets. Nothing special; the bar is short enough that an iPad in portrait gets the desktop treatment. The switch to the tab bar is at the existing 768px breakpoint, in CSS, so both shells are in the DOM and there is no hydration flash.
  • Admins. No extra entry. "Edit desk info" stays in the desk sheet, where the desk is.

Phones (under 768px)

  • The bar. Four items: Desks, Bookings, Metrics, You. 22px icon over a 10px label, 49px tall plus the home-indicator inset, translucent paper with blur and a hairline on top. Active: ink label, moss icon. Nothing else on screen belongs to the shell; the page title is the first thing you see.
  • Scrolling. The page already scrolls inside the sheet stack outlet on phones; the bar is pinned to the bottom of that outlet, above the scrolling content, and pages get bottom padding equal to the bar so the last block of desks clears it. Tapping the active tab again scrolls its page to the top.
  • Sheets. The desk sheet rises over the bar. The depth effect pushes the whole screen back, bar included, because the bar lives inside the outlet's scaled layer. While a sheet is up the bar is inert, as the rest of the page is.
  • Keyboard. When an input has focus (Edit desk, Edit profile, the code field on login) the bar hides, so it never rides up on top of the keyboard. It returns when focus leaves.
  • Safari's own bar. Safari on iPhone puts its address bar at the bottom too. A flush, full-width tab bar is the same shape, so the two stack into one toolbar, which is how every web app that does this looks. Add a web manifest with display: standalone so people who put the app on their home screen get the bar on the home indicator alone.
  • Secondary pages keep the bar with the parent tab lit and show the "← Desks" (or "← Bookings") link above the title, so there is never a screen with no way back. Reserve opens as a bottom sheet from the desk anyway; the page form is for direct links.
  • Signed out. Login and register have no bar, as now.
  • Landscape. The bar stays at the bottom with labels; four items fit easily.

Touches that work with any direction

Small things that separate "redesigned" from "rebuilt properly". Each is a half-day or less.

Reserve for today, one tap

On someone else's free desk the sheet's only button is "Reserve for today", and it submits without leaving the page. The desk flips to "yours" in place. Booking ahead stays limited to your own desk.

Your desk always has a home

The desk view pins a small "Your desk" card above the map: its status today, and a one-tap "Release today" if you're not coming in. That's the most common action for assigned users.

Live free count in the header

"26 of 40 free" updates as you change the day. It's the number people check before deciding whether to come in.

Initials avatars with stable colours

Hash the user ID to one of eight muted hues. The same person is always the same colour across the map, the sheet, and My reservations.

Optimistic desk state

Reserving flips the desk immediately and rolls back with a toast if the server says no. Today the page waits for a full round trip and a redirect.

Sheet remembers your last desk

Re-opening the desk view scrolls to and highlights the desk you last interacted with, so the "book, check, book again" loop is short.

Dark mode for real

The tokens already exist in globals.css but nothing toggles them. Add a system-preference default and a switch in the sidebar footer next to logout.

Logout that closes the loop

A confirm popover on the sidebar item, then the login page shows "You're signed out" for one visit. No silent redirect.

Empty and error states with a next step

"No free desks on Thursday. Try Friday or the aisle." Error card gets a retry and a "report a bug" link that pre-fills the route.

Considered and set aside

Ink & Clay (warm ink, clay accent) was the closest runner-up but clay on white fails AA at small sizes, so it could only ever be a fill. Graphite & Cobalt read too much like a generic tool. Both are one token swap away if you change your mind; the tile, panel and forms don't care which accent they get.

Sheets and modals: Silk

Silk (@silk-hq/components, currently 0.10.x, React 17 to 19, no other dependencies) replaces vaul and the Radix dialog for every overlay. It is unstyled, so the tokens above apply as they do everywhere else, and it handles the parts vaul never did well: trackpad and mouse swipes, the on-screen keyboard, iOS back-swipe, and stacking. How each surface maps:

  • Desk detail. One Sheet.Root, with contentPlacement switched by the existing 768px media query: bottom on phones, right on desktop. On phones two detents: a peek showing the name, today's status and the button, and a full detent revealing the two-week grid. Swipe to dismiss on both.
  • Reserve form on phones. Opens as a full-height bottom sheet from "Book days" on your own desk, so the map stays underneath. On desktop it remains the page it is today.
  • Confirmations. "Remove reservation", "Stop and remove" and the admin "Unassign desk" become a small centred sheet with sheetRole="alertdialog" and swipe={false}, so they cannot be dismissed by accident.
  • Long content. The sheet body uses Silk's Scroll so the grid scrolls inside the sheet and the handle stays put.
  • Sidebar. Replaced under Chrome. Options 1 to 3 have no drawer at all; option 4's phone drawer is a Silk side sheet. Either way the shadcn sidebar and @radix-ui/react-dialog go along with vaul.

Licence. Silk requires a license prop on every root, and the docs state that commercial use needs a commercial licence. An internal company tool most likely counts, so confirm the terms and cost on silkhq.com before this step starts; I could not read the pricing page.
